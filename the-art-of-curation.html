The Art of Curation

[placeholder date] · On Curating

Taking a photograph and building a gallery are two different crafts. One happens in a moment, in front of a subject. The other happens much later, often alone, deciding which of those moments deserve to stand next to each other. A strong gallery isn't just a pile of good images — it's a sequence of choices about what to include, what to leave out, and what story the collection tells when it's viewed as a whole.

Editing Is Part of the Work

Out of any extended body of work, only a small fraction earns a place in a final gallery. That editing-down process is uncomfortable — it means setting aside technically fine images because they don't add anything new, or because they repeat a mood another photo already covers. A gallery isn't meant to show everything that was captured. It's meant to show only what deserves a second look, which is a much shorter list.

Sequence Changes Meaning

The same photograph can read differently depending on what sits beside it. A quiet portrait placed after a loud, busy frame feels like a pause. The same portrait placed between two similarly quiet images can feel flat instead. Curating a gallery means thinking in rhythm, not just quality — alternating tension and stillness, wide and close, color and shadow, so that moving through the collection feels intentional rather than random.

A Gallery Is a Point of View

What separates a curated body of work from a photo dump is that it argues for something — a way of seeing, a recurring subject, a consistent use of light. Viewers may never consciously notice the pattern, but they feel its absence when it's missing. That's really what curation is: not selecting the "best" photos in isolation, but selecting the photos that, together, say something a single image couldn't say on its own.

Every gallery on this site has gone through that same process — shot, set aside, revisited, and narrowed down to what felt worth keeping. If you'd like to see more of that work, or talk about a project of your own, I'd love to hear from you.
